A

Problem statement and project

Problem statement 1 (course brief): your company has just won a contract for the construction of a shopping mall of 7 blocks with interconnected pathways and 3 storeys of underground parking. The preliminary estimate is about 12,000 m³ of concrete, and the blocks are built on 8 acres of land; the mobilisation amount arrives within a week brief.

The theme is to complete all paperwork for the local governing body and RERA, develop a detailed site layout for efficient circulation of labour housing, material storage and the site office, and determine the total duration of the project.

Objectives and key questions

Project objectives (from PS1)

  1. ApprovalsComplete all statutory documentation and approvals for the local governing body and RERA.
  2. Concreting systemDevelop an economical and efficient concreting system that improves productivity, reduces cost and minimises material wastage.
  3. Testing labSet up a material testing laboratory on site so that materials meet the relevant standards.
  4. Site layoutPrepare a detailed layout for efficient circulation and use of labour shelters, material storage, site offices, equipment and other temporary facilities.
  5. DurationDetermine the overall project duration through a construction schedule for the major activities.

Questions the group set out to answer

  • What paperwork must be in place before construction starts?
  • What is the RERA Act, and when was the bill proposed?
  • Which facilities must be provided for labourers on site, and are government rules for labour camps followed?
  • What data must be maintained on site?
  • RMC or an on-site batching plant for this project?
  • Which tests must be carried out on site, and what equipment does the testing lab need?
  • Is the building's budget sanctioned in parts, and is it uniform?
  • How many labourers will stay on site?
  • How will entry and exit at the gates be controlled?
  • Is PPE (helmet, shoes, vest) and first aid available?
  • How will pathways be kept clear for movement?
  • How will electricity and water reach the site and labour camp?
  • How will waste disposal and drainage be managed?

Site layout — two phases

PS1 plans the 8-acre plot in two phases, with the site between an existing property on the west and a park on the east. Gate 1 is the entry and Gate 2 the exit, giving one-way traffic through the site PS1 drawing.

Phase 1 — blocks 1 to 5

  • Blocks 1–5 under construction
  • Material storage room and security office beside Gate 1 (entry)
  • Material testing lab, site office and sales office in a row near the entry
  • Labour camp with water and toilet on the east side, near Gate 1
  • Two dumping areas for excavated soil between the blocks

Phase 2 — blocks 6 and 7

  • Blocks 1–5 complete; blocks 6 and 7 under construction
  • Site office, sales office, testing lab, labour camp and security office move north, beside Gate 2
  • Material storage, water and the soil dumping area move between blocks 4 and 7
  • Toilet beside block 3 / 4

F

RERA and statutory approvals

The RERA Act

  • The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 came into force on 1 May 2017
  • It regulates the real estate sector and promotes transparency, accountability and efficiency in transactions
  • It protects homebuyers and pushes projects to finish within committed timelines
  • It requires transparent project information, approvals and finances

Approvals are also needed from the local governing body — the Hubballi-Dharwad Municipal Corporation (HDMC) PS1.

What a promoter must furnish for RERA registration

Labour welfare facilities

Facilities listed in PS1

  • Safe, hygienic drinking water
  • Sanitation and toilets; washing and bathing
  • Rest shelters and accommodation where required
  • First aid and basic medical help
  • Canteen or suitable food facilities
  • Lighting and ventilation; clean living and working areas
  • Waste disposal and drainage
  • Safety equipment and PPE
  • Changing rooms and storage for belongings
  • Facilities for women workers and childcare, where applicable

What the law requires

  • BOCW Act, 1996 — s.32 drinking water
  • s.33 latrines and urinals
  • s.34 accommodation
  • Karnataka BOCW Rules, 2006
  • Crèche and first aid where applicable verify

Review notes — checks on the PS1 figures

  • Arithmetic checked: 4,200 × 12,000 = ₹5.04 crore; + ₹50 lakh of equipment and set-up = ₹5.54 crore; RMC 5,500 × 12,000 = ₹6.60 crore; saving ₹1.06 crore ✓
  • The comparison is not like-for-like: the RMC rate includes labour and pumping, while the on-site figure has no batching-plant labour, power, water, loader or QC staff verify
  • A single rate is used for all 12,000 m³; a grade-wise split (and mix design) would change the material cost
  • Any resale value of the plant and pump at the end of the project is not counted
  • The comparison is a preliminary academic exercise, not a confirmed saving

Conclusion

PS1 sets the baseline for every later sheet: an 8-acre, seven-block mall with three parking levels and about 12,000 m³ of concrete, built in two phases with a one-way entry-to-exit route, a site testing lab and a labour camp kept clear of the work fronts. On the deck's figures on-site batching is about ₹1.06 crore cheaper than RMC (₹5.54 vs ₹6.60 crore), but the comparison leaves out plant labour and running costs, so it needs a like-for-like recalculation before it is relied on.

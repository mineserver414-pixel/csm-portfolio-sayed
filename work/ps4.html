Problem statement

Official theme

To reduce wastage of steel and formwork through efficient management.

Your company’s historical data shows the average wastage of steel has been 12% and plywood for formwork wastage has been around 9%. Excavation is in progress for the next 30 days. Use this time to develop a system to have a considerable amount of control over –

  1. The calculation of steel required (BBS)

  2. The process of ordering the steel (project-wise or block-wise)

  3. Storage and releasing it to the fabricators

  4. Reconciliation

Please plan and execute your system to bring the wastage of steel down to 5 %.

  1. A similar system is to be set up for formwork (reduce wastage by 4 %) –

  2. Figure out the different groups of columns and beams based on size.

  3. Set up a working procedure to reuse a set of formwork for a minimum of 10 times.

Steel wastage — definition

Issued steel compared with the theoretical BBS quantity, accounting for reusable offcuts, returns, scrap and stock balance.

Wastage % = verified scrap and loss ÷ net issued × 100

Formwork wastage — definition

Damaged / irrecoverable plywood area as a share of plywood area issued in the period (area basis, m²). Cost-based reporting can follow once rates are known.

Formwork wastage % = irrecoverable area ÷ area issued × 100

Technical approach

FIGURE Steel wastage-control workflow — Conceptual / proposed arrangement

Steel controls

  • Accurate BBS with bar-mark verification against drawings
  • Cutting and bending schedules grouped by diameter
  • Standard stock lengths with nested cutting patterns
  • Reuse of suitable offcuts only where technically permitted
  • Storage by diameter / grade, tagged; protection from corrosion
  • Control of unauthorised cutting, theft and handling damage
  • Substitutions, laps and anchorages only with the structural designer's approval — never changed just to cut waste

Formwork controls

  • Panel modularisation and standard dimensions
  • Cutting plans and reuse planning
  • Careful stripping; no crowbars on faces
  • Cleaning, release agent, edge sealing, moisture protection
  • Flat stacking on battens
  • Repair-and-reuse assessment; reject damaged or unsafe panels
  • Cycle log for every set

Monitoring

A weekly dashboard plots steel wastage % by diameter and plywood wastage % against the 5% lines, using reconciled data only — no chart is shown until real data exists. Above target → root cause, corrective action and a toolbox talk.

Limitations

  • No tonnage, sizes, plywood quantity or measured wastage is stated.
  • The plywood target interpretation needs faculty confirmation.
  • The cost example is illustrative.

Key findings and learning outcomes

  • Wastage figures mean nothing without a fixed definition and denominator.
  • Block-wise call-off keeps stock small and reconciliation simple.
  • Formwork life is decided at stripping and stacking, not at purchase.

Conclusion

Steel waste is controlled by an approved BBS, nested cutting, offcut reuse, tagged storage and a weekly material balance; plywood waste by member grouping, numbered panel sets and a cycle log aiming at ten or more uses. The 30 excavation days are used to set up these systems and a baseline, so progress towards 5% can be shown with reconciled data.
